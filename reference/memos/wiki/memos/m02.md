**From** [Chair, Circular Economy Taskforce] **to** [Director of
Growth and Productivity, HM Treasury]. Raw:
[memo](../../raw/m02-uk-plastic-recycling.md),
[feedback](../../raw/m02-feedback.md).

## In a minute

UK firms can't get enough verified, affordable UK-made recycled plastic to meet
packaging rules, because UK recyclers' energy costs make them dearer than
imports. The memo asks the Treasury to spend the plastic packaging tax's
revenue (£259M in 2024) on cutting recyclers' energy costs until 2030.

## Checks

- **How much of the gap the recommendation closes.** It would add 300 to 700kt
  against a 1,650 to 2,500kt shortfall: between 12% and 42%. The memo never
  says this, and calls it a way to "close the supply-demand loop".
- **What the subsidy costs.** It never says. The £259M figure is the funding
  source, and the reader is left to assume a 15 to 25% energy-cost cut fits
  inside it.
- **"Conservative".** Endnote xxxii says the estimate leaves out economies of
  scale, import costs and transport. Those pull in different directions, so
  "conservative" is a judgement the memo doesn't show.
- **The shortfall's upper bound** (2,500kt) is larger than total plastic waste
  (2,265kt, endnote xiv). The endnote attributes the difference to projected
  growth. That holds, but it should be in the body.
- **0.02% of total government spending**: £259M against about £1.2tn. Checks.

## Moves worth keeping

- **Put the problem in the reader's frame.** For a Treasury growth director,
  compliance becomes "an investment trigger", and the risk is "the perception
  of creating barriers to growth".
- **Pay for it from money the reader already controls.** A marker said
  anchoring the recommendation to an existing revenue stream makes it feasible.
- **Analyse the existing instruments before proposing new ones.** The packaging
  tax and EPR are each shown to fix one side of the market and not the other.
  A marker credited this as diagnosing a structural mismatch.
- **Show your working in the endnotes**, with what it leaves out.
