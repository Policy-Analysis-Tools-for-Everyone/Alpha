## In a minute

More people are sleeping rough in Somerville's main square, partly because of
movement along the Red Line and a shelter closure next door, and residents are
angry. Housing all 150 homeless people would cost about $4M a year, about 1% of
the city budget. Do that now with the local homelessness charity, and start a
regional pact with Boston and Cambridge, because the problem moves between
cities.

## Checks

- **The arithmetic holds.** $2,200 × 12 × 150 = $3.96M. $4M is 1.04% of $380M.
- **The charity's half doesn't get checked.** The charity's $2M is 24% of its
  $8.4M budget. "A drop in the bucket" is true of the city's half only.
- **Housing First, contradicted.** PSH is defined as housing with support, then
  listed as a con because it "does not solve for co-morbidities". The cost
  covers rent only, not the support.
- **The regional handover.** "Funds appropriated from option one can then be
  slowly diverted into the regional initiative … ensuring continuity of care."
  Diverting the money that pays for permanent leases ends the leases. The
  sentence claims the opposite of what it describes.
- **One organisation, two names.** "Somerville Housing Coalition" in the
  abbreviations, "Somerville Homeless Coalition" in the cover note.
- **No references** in the held copy.

## Moves worth keeping

- **Use the decision maker's own words as the constraint.** "You yourself have
  said that we need to maintain the city's compassionate,
  non-criminilisation policy while delivering the tangible improvements
  residents demand."
- **One cost-to-budget ratio a reader can check.** $4M against $380M.
- **Sequence a quick fix and a structural one, and tie the second to the
  cause.** Local housing now, a regional pact because the problem moves along
  the Red Line.
- **Options that differ in theory of change**, not in size.
- **Use the new mayor's political capital as feasibility.** A marker picked it
  out.
