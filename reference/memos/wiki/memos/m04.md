# M04: Korea petrochemicals

**From** Senior Research Fellow, KIET **to** Director, Chemical
Industry Team, MOTI. Raw: [memo](../../raw/m04-korea-petrochemicals.md),
[feedback](../../raw/m04-feedback.md).

## In a minute

Korea's petrochemical firms are locked into making cheap bulk chemicals that no
longer pay, and the new Special Act helps them shut plants but not move into
better products. MOTI should make state-bank lending conditional on investing
in high-value, low-carbon products, publish a roadmap, and fund pilots.

## Checks

- **No numbers on Option 2.** No cost, no investment shift, no jobs. A marker asked
  for a cost-benefit view, even with rough estimates.
- **The coordination contradiction**, quoted under R13.
- **Reference 6** (the Bank of Korea outlook) supports both R&D intensity and
  Yeosu's employment share. One may be misattributed.
- **Reference 5** (2016) supports a claim about 2015 to 2021.
- **Reference 3's URL** begins `https://https://`.
- **Utilisation (94% to 74%) and margins (13.4% to 0.6%)** come from a news
  article (reference 4), the weakest source for the memo's headline figures.

## Moves worth keeping

- **One mechanism, several levels, each answered by a design feature.**
  Conditional finance answers firm incentives and risk-averse banks; the
  coordination mechanism answers fragmentation.
- **A one-line frame that does work.** "Structural rather than cyclical" tells
  the reader why the usual response won't do.
- **A separate why-now section.** The capacity cut frees resources that will go
  somewhere.
- **Criteria as questions.**
- **Put the recipient's own constraint in Stakeholders.**
- **Order implementation by the budget calendar.**
