# Patterns

What recurs across the corpus. Mined from `memos/` and `../raw/*-feedback.md`.
Re-mine whenever a memo is added; `../README.md` has the steps.

**Read the caveats first.** 5 memos, all chosen as strong, from one module. Counts say what strong memos in this module have in common.
They don't say what earns marks, and they don't generalise past the module
without more evidence. Each pattern names its kind of judgement: marker,
rubric or check.

## P1. Every memo breaks its own chain somewhere

**5 of 5. Rubric and check.** In each memo there is a place where the
recommendation doesn't follow from the memo's own analysis:

| Memo | The break |
|---|---|
| M01 | The Option 2 evaluated in the table is not the Option 2 recommended |
| M02 | Import verification is diagnosed as a cause; nothing recommended touches it. Marker 2 finds a second: the public and local authorities |
| M03 | Diverting the lease money to the regional pact is said to ensure "continuity of care" |
| M04 | The analysis says government can't coordinate; the recommendation relies on coordination as a strength |
| M05 | The first driver diagnosed is answered only by an instrument in the rejected option |

The markers named 1 of these 5 (M02's). These are the strongest memos in the
cohort. The breaks are invisible in reading order and visible only when the
issue, the analysis, the option table and the recommendation are held side by
side, which is exactly what an agent can do and a tired reader can't.

**Why it matters for `story`:** its self-check has "the recommendation follows
from the underlying analysis", and the method has "upstream returns". Neither
says how to check it. A concrete test (every driver answered or set aside; the
recommended option is the one evaluated) would have caught all 5.

## P2. Risks is the weakest section in every memo

**5 of 5. Rubric.** 3 memos have no risks section, and in 2 it is weak. M02's is
26 words saying the industry is volatile. M04's names real risks, then turns
into a case for acting now. None says what would reduce an uncertainty, which
the brief asks for in so many words.

**Marker:** the markers raised risks only for M03, and only as content: a
risk the memo should have had (success drawing people to the city) and
hope where evidence should be. None asked for a risks section.

**Why it matters:** `story` already prescribes the right form (*`[uncertainty]`
matters because `[consequence]`; we would reduce it through `[test]`*). The
corpus says even strong human writers skip it, so an agent that does it well
adds something real. M03's marker shows which risk was missing: one produced by
the recommended option's own mechanism.

## P3. The opening is where strong memos are strongest

**5 of 5 put a magnitude in the opening paragraph of the memo proper; 4 of 5
rate Strong on R2. Rubric and marker.** Markers praised the problem statement
in M02, M04 and M05 unprompted. The common move is a number, then why it
matters *to this reader's own agenda*: growth (M02), the mayor's own words
(M03), the Special Act's gap (M04), the minister's mission (M05).

## P4. The recommendation is always easy to find

**5 of 5. Rubric.** Every memo opens its recommendation section with the
answer. M05 is the partial case: its executive summary "recommends" a
necessity, not an action, so the answer appears only after the options.

## P5. The recommended option rarely carries its own numbers

**Check.** Of the 5 recommendations:

| | States its cost | States how much of the problem it fixes |
|---|---|---|
| M01 | Yes ($1.53M moved, $3M co-funded) | No |
| M02 | No (names the funding source, not the cost) | Yes (300 to 700kt), but not as a share of the gap, which is 12 to 42% |
| M03 | Yes ($4M a year) | Yes (150 people housed) |
| M04 | No | No |
| M05 | No | No |

**Marker:** a M04 marker asked for a cost-benefit view, even with rough
estimates, and the M03 markers for evidence in place of hope. The memos that the markers asked for
numbers are the ones without them.

## P6. Options are bundles

**4 of 5 recommend a bundle of instruments. Rubric and marker.** M01 (3 parts),
M03 (2 options combined), M04 (roadmap, conditional finance, pilot
infrastructure), M05 (plan, employment service, conditions). Only M02
recommends one instrument.

**Marker:** both M05 markers made the options their main criticism: Option 2
was too abstract to see why it won, and 2 bundles were too few for measures
with such different lead times. A M01 marker found that memo dense for trying to
do too much. A bundle hides which part
does the work, and in M05 it is how a rejected option took the answer to the
first driver with it (P1).

## P7. Strong memos write to the reader's agenda, and markers notice when they miss it

**5 of 5 markers' comments turn on reader fit. Marker.** Praised: M02 framed in
the growth mission, M03 aligned to a new mayor's capital, M05 the gap between
ambition and workforce made plain. Criticised: M04 gives an expert reader
too much context and too little on how; M01 is too dense.

It is the theme the markers return to most, and the rubric under-weights it:
R14 (proportion) is one criterion of 15. See `tensions.md` T9.

## P8. The answer is to redirect or condition money already there

**4 of 5. Rubric.** M01 moves an existing budget line. M02 spends an existing
tax's revenue. M04 attaches conditions to existing policy finance. M05 attaches
"non-price" conditions to existing funding. M03 alone proposes new spending.

This is the module's frame (market shaping, conditionality), and a sensible
move for a reader who controls a budget. It is also exactly the kind of pattern
that would make an agent trained on these memos recommend conditionality by
default. See `tensions.md` T6.

## P9. Markers and the rubric see different things

**Marker against rubric, all 5 memos.**

| Markers saw, the rubric missed | Rubric and checks saw, markers didn't mention |
|---|---|
| Why film, not other arts? (M01) What do the innovation leaders do? (M04) Both are benchmarking | 4 of the 5 chain breaks (P1) |
| Success drawing more homeless people to Somerville (M03): a second-order effect | Arithmetic and coverage: M02's 12 to 42%, M05's 300,000 |
| The spillover benefits that would decide Option I against IV (M03) | Risks sections (P2) |
| The political reading of housing people in a housing crisis (M03) | Sourcing: M03's missing references, M04's and M05's |
| Whether context fits an expert reader (M04) | Writing tics under `writing.md` |

The left column is judgement about the world of the decision. The right column
is internal consistency and checkable fact. An agent is better placed for the
right and needs the left from the user. Both matter, and the rubric needs
criteria for the left (`tensions.md` T7).

## P10. Writing controls are not what markers reward

**Marker against rubric.** No marker commented on vocabulary, dashes or
transitions. M05, with "mission-critical" twice, "pivotal" and 7 em dashes, had
its writing praised; M01 has the most `writing.md` hits and its writing was
praised too. Markers praise clarity, signposting and tightness.

`writing.md` exists to stop machine voice, and a reader who spots it stops
trusting the document, so it stays. But it is not what makes these memos
strong, and the skill should spend effort on P1, P2 and P5 before polish.

## P11. The 8 sections are a guide, and judgement decides

**Check and marker.** Sections of the brief's 8 present: M04 8, M01 7, M02 7,
M05 6, M03 4. All 5 are among the strongest in the cohort, and M03, with the
fewest sections, drew some of the warmest feedback. The brief itself says
sections may be longer, shorter or omitted.

What the strong memos share is not a set of headings but a choice about this
reader. M03 spends 45% of its words on options for a mayor who knows the city;
M04 spends a third on analysis for a director who knows the industry, and its
marker said so. Each omission or expansion had a cost the writer could have
seen: M03's missing stakeholder section is where its markers found the
opposition it hadn't reckoned with.

So the useful question for a writer is not "are all 8 sections there?" but
"what does each section do for this reader, and what is lost by leaving it
out?" That is critical thinking the analysis has to support, and a checklist
can't. See `tensions.md` T2.

## P12. Cover notes carry the authorising environment, and sometimes the evidence

**4 of 5 have a cover note (M05's held copy doesn't). Marker and check.** M02's
was praised for setting out the authorising environment. M01's is 26% of its
words and holds the evidence for the case, which the memo's reader never sees.
See `tensions.md` T1.
