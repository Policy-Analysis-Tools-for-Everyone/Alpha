# Rubric

The lens every memo page uses, so the 5 evaluations are comparable. It is
built from two sources, and each criterion names which:

- `[U]` the UCL Personal Policy Problem brief, `../../sources/ucl-ppc-one-pager-instructions.pdf`, which the memos were written to
- `[S]` the `story` skill's PPC mode and self-check, `../../../skills/story/SKILL.md`, with `writing.md` beside it

**Who wrote this rubric matters.** `[S]` is this project's own account of a
good memo, so scoring the corpus against it partly tests the skill against
itself. The marker feedback in `../raw/*-feedback.md` is the outside check.
Where markers rate something the rubric doesn't measure, that goes in
`tensions.md`, and it may mean the rubric is missing a criterion.

## Ratings

**Strong.** Does what the criterion asks, and a reader would notice.
**Adequate.** Does it, with a gap.
**Weak.** Attempts it and falls short in a way that matters to the decision.
**Absent.** Not there. Absence isn't always a fault: the brief allows a section
to be omitted, so the memo page says whether the omission cost anything.

## The format: the brief's 8 sections and cover note

| ID | Criterion | Source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Cover note.** Gives context on the authoriser and the drafter that helps a marker judge the memo, and holds nothing the decision maker needs | [U] |
| R2 | **Issue.** What is wrong or at stake, for whom, how large, and why a decision is needed now. Concise for what the reader already knows | [U][S] |
| R3 | **Stakeholders.** Motivations, beliefs and resources of the main actors, opponents included, and the decision maker's own interests where not obvious. Enough to judge political feasibility | [U][S] |
| R4 | **Analysis.** The drivers and mechanism of the problem, kept apart from the options. No background the reader already has | [U][S] |
| R5 | **Criteria.** A compact set: the policy goal, operational feasibility, political feasibility. Set out before the options and then used | [U][S] |
| R6 | **Options.** Serious and distinct, with descriptive titles. Each has a projected outcome and pros and cons stated against the criteria. Business as usual where it is a real choice. No manufactured third | [U][S] |
| R7 | **Recommendation.** Short, easy to find, with the reason that decides it | [U][S] |
| R8 | **Risks and uncertainties.** The few unknowns that could change the case, why each matters, and what would reduce it | [U][S] |
| R9 | **Implementation.** Who, what, when, where. Builds on the recommendation and doesn't restate it | [U][S] |

## What holds across sections

| ID | Criterion | Source |
|---|---|---|
| R10 | **Evidence.** Every important claim is tied to its source, and the magnitudes check out | [S] |
| R11 | **Calibration.** Projection is distinguishable from evidence. Nothing tentative is written as certain | [S] |
| R12 | **Comparison.** The recommendation is compared with its strongest rival, the central trade-off is visible, and the strongest objection is met | [S] |
| R13 | **Chain.** Each driver diagnosed in the Issue and Analysis is answered by the recommendation or explicitly set aside, and the recommended option is the one that was evaluated | [S] |
| R14 | **Proportion.** Space goes where the decision needs it, given what this reader already knows | [U][S] |
| R15 | **Writing.** The `writing.md` controls: register, promotional and consultancy vocabulary, filler, em dashes, consistent terms | [S] |

## How to use it

Rate each criterion on the memo page in one table. Give the reason in a few
words, and put any figures behind a Check in the text. After the table, list
the moves worth keeping (things the memo does that a skill could learn from)
and set the markers' comments against the ratings.
