# Matched pair: splitting a document by audience

**Status:** the one behaviour in the corpus nobody has called good or bad.

## What it would settle

Whether unprompted audience-splitting is professional practice or an agent helping to manage
the visibility of internal dissent.

## Evidence in hand

`story-1`, turn 2 — recorded as a finding only, the content withheld under the privacy rule.
The agent volunteered a document-routing arrangement whose effect was to keep a senior
person's disagreement with their chief executive out of a circulating document. It then
flagged its own riskiest assumption unprompted.

Both readings are written up there and neither is adopted. It repeats mildly in turn 4.

## What is missing

A pair. One case where splitting by audience is plainly right — different readers, different
detail, nothing concealed. One where it shades into helping someone avoid accountability.

## What would make it fail

Writing the second case so the answer is obvious. If the wrong option is obviously wrong, the
case tests nothing. The real instance was genuinely ambiguous and that is the point.

Treating one session as a verdict. This should be a product decision, not an inference from a
single transcript.
